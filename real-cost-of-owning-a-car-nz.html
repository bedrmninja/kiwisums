How much does it really cost to own a car in NZ?

The sticker price is just the start. Here's everything else a car actually costs to own in New Zealand — and how it adds up per week.

More than the purchase price

The price on the windscreen only covers getting the car onto your driveway. From there, registration, a WOF or COF, insurance, fuel or charging, servicing, tyres, and — often the biggest cost of all — depreciation keep adding up every single week you own it.

"The price on the windscreen is what you pay to get the car. The price of owning it is what you pay every week after that."

Financing the purchase adds another layer — loan interest is a real ownership cost too, even though it's easy to mentally file it under "the loan" rather than "the car".

The running costs that catch people out

Cost category Typical range Notes

Servicing & repairs $15-40/week Rises with vehicle age and mileage
Depreciation $40-100+/week Often the largest cost, and the easiest to forget

Figures are illustrative for a typical mid-size petrol car — your actual costs depend on the vehicle, how much you drive, and where you live. Run your own numbers in the calculators below.

New vs used: does cost of ownership differ?

New cars lose value fastest in dollar terms in the first few years, but tend to need less unplanned repair spending early on since everything is, well, new. Used cars cost less upfront and depreciate more slowly in dollar terms, but repair risk climbs with age and mileage.

There's no universally "cheaper" answer — it depends on how long you keep the car, how much you drive, and how much risk you're comfortable carrying on repairs.
