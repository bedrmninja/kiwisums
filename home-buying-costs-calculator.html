Costs first-home buyers often forget

Most first-home buyers budget carefully for their deposit, then get caught out by the additional cash needed on top of it. Legal fees, a building inspection, and a LIM report are the big three that apply to almost every purchase, commonly totalling $3,000-$4,000 combined. A registered valuation may be needed if your bank doesn't accept an automated valuation model, particularly for older, unusual, or higher-value properties.

Beyond the transaction itself, budget for moving costs and a buffer for anything that needs immediate attention once you're in — a $400 building inspection doesn't catch everything, and older homes especially can have surprises in the first few months.

Frequently asked questions

Legal/conveyancing fees ($1,500-$2,500), a building inspection ($400-$900), a LIM report ($250-$450), a registered valuation if required ($600-$1,200), moving costs, and sometimes a loan establishment fee. Altogether commonly $3,000-$6,000.

Neither is legally required, but both are strongly recommended. A building inspection checks the physical condition; a LIM report reveals council records like consents, rates and hazards. Skipping them to save money is a common source of expensive surprises.
