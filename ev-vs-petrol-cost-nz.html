EV vs petrol: which is cheaper to own in NZ in 2026?

Electric cars cost more upfront but less to run. Here's how the total cost of ownership actually compares in New Zealand.

The upfront price gap

EVs generally still carry a higher purchase price than an equivalent petrol model, though the gap has been narrowing as more affordable EV models arrive and battery costs continue to fall. Used EV prices have also dropped meaningfully as more come off early leases.

Running cost comparison

On a per-100km basis, charging at home is typically far cheaper than filling up with petrol — often a third or less of the cost, depending on your power plan and driving style. Public fast-charging narrows that gap significantly, sometimes closer to petrol prices.

"The fuel savings are real — the question is whether they add up fast enough to close the upfront price gap for your driving pattern."

Servicing tends to be cheaper for EVs too — no oil changes, fewer moving parts, less to wear out — though tyres can wear faster due to the extra weight of the battery, and any battery-related repairs outside warranty can be expensive.

Where EVs claw it back

The more you drive, the faster fuel and servicing savings add up — high-mileage drivers (long commutes, high-km households) tend to recoup an EV's price premium fastest. Home solar can push the running cost advantage even further.

Where petrol still wins

For low-mileage drivers, occasional long road trips without easy charging access, or anyone without off-street parking to install a home charger, the running-cost advantage shrinks and petrol can still come out ahead on total cost, especially for a car kept only a few years.

Which suits your driving?

How much do you drive each year?

With lower annual driving, it'll likely take longer to recoup an EV's higher upfront price through fuel savings alone — the maths still might work, but it's worth modelling carefully. Check the EV vs petrol calculator.

What you can do now

  • Model your exact driving pattern. Annual km makes a big difference — use the EV vs petrol calculator.
  • Check your charging setup. Off-street parking and a home charger change the maths substantially versus relying on public charging.
  • Compare full running costs. See the car running cost calculator to add rego, insurance and servicing into the picture.
  • Check finance costs too. A higher purchase price often means a bigger loan — see the car finance calculator.
